AI on IBM Z

Deploy Models

Deployment is where a trained and optimized model moves from a development environment into production — where it can process real data and generate business value. On IBM Z, deployment means co-locating your model with the mission-critical data and applications it needs to score: Db2 transactions, CICS workflows, IMS applications, and more. That proximity is the key to real-time, in-transaction AI at the low latencies that enterprise workloads demand.

IBM Z supports multiple deployment paths depending on your operating environment, your model type, and the kind of application you're infusing AI into.

What happens at this stage?

Move to production

Transfer your trained, optimized model from a development environment to a production runtime — on z/OS, Linux on Z, or an OpenShift container environment.

Register and version the model

Register the model in a lifecycle management platform so it can be tracked, versioned, and governed through its production life.

Configure scoring endpoints

Set up the model to receive inference requests — via REST API, CICS/IMS native scoring, batch jobs, or a gRPC serving endpoint.

Validate in production

Run test traffic through the deployed model to confirm it produces expected results before enabling it for live workloads.

Who is this stage for?

Deployment is primarily owned by IT Architects and Application Architects, with ML Engineers responsible for the model artifact and deployment tooling. Data Scientists are stakeholders who validate that the deployed model performs as expected. Line of Business stakeholders set the production SLAs and scoring latency requirements.

Where to Start

Your deployment path depends primarily on your target operating environment and how you need to integrate AI into your applications. Use this to find your path:

Deploying on z/OS — in-transaction AI

Your business applications run on z/OS (CICS, IMS, Batch) and you want AI scoring co-located with transaction data — near-zero latency, no data movement.

→ Use Machine Learning for IBM z/OS (MLz). The full-featured ML platform on z/OS that deploys models trained anywhere and scores them natively inside CICS, IMS, and batch workflows with Telum acceleration.

Deploying on Linux on Z or LinuxONE — containerized

Your AI workloads run in containers on Linux on Z or LinuxONE, and you want a flexible, cloud-native deployment environment with MLOps capabilities.

→ Use Red Hat OpenShift AI. A Kubernetes-native AI/ML platform that deploys models across any OpenShift footprint — including IBM Z and LinuxONE — with built-in pipeline orchestration, model serving, and monitoring.

Deploying foundation models and LLMs

You're deploying a large language model or generative AI application on IBM Z, using IBM Spyre or Telum II for acceleration.

→ Use watsonx.ai or Red Hat OpenShift AI to manage the model lifecycle, and the Red Hat AI Inference Server (vLLM-based) or AI Optimizer for IBM Z as the serving infrastructure for LLM inference.

Hybrid cloud — train anywhere, deploy on Z

You trained a model in the cloud or on a GPU cluster and want to bring it to IBM Z for accelerated production inference.

→ Convert to ONNX or PMML (in the Optimize stage), then import directly into MLz or deploy via the AI Toolkit container images. Both paths accept models trained on any platform.

Machine Learning for IBM z/OS (MLz)

Machine Learning for IBM z/OS is the full-featured machine learning platform on z/OS, designed to infuse AI into mission-critical IBM Z enterprise applications. It deploys models trained anywhere — or on IBM Z itself — directly on z/OS, co-located with enterprise applications, transaction data, and business logic. The result is high-throughput, extremely low latency AI scoring that modernizes z/OS applications without disrupting SLAs.

Why deploy with MLz

The defining advantage of MLz is data and application co-location. Your model runs on the same system as your transactions — no round-trip to an external inference server, no data movement off-platform. This is what makes sub-millisecond, in-transaction scoring at billions of operations per day possible on IBM Z.

Red Hat OpenShift AI

Red Hat OpenShift AI (RHOAI) is a Kubernetes-native AI/ML platform that brings end-to-end MLOps to IBM Z and LinuxONE. It runs alongside traditional z/OS applications and VMs on the same IBM Z system, deploying containerized AI workloads with enterprise-grade infrastructure support — including IBM Telum processors and IBM Spyre PCIe accelerator cards.

What RHOAI gives you on IBM Z

  • Unified deployment platform — Deploy models across any OpenShift footprint: on-premises IBM Z, LinuxONE, hybrid cloud, and virtual machines — all from one platform
  • Built-in MLOps — Model development, deployment, monitoring, and lifecycle management in one environment; expand DevOps practices to AI/ML
  • Model serving at scale — Integrated with KServe, Triton Inference Server, and vLLM for flexible serving of both predictive and generative AI models
  • Telum and Spyre acceleration — Run containerized AI workloads alongside traditional applications and leverage IBM Z hardware accelerators for high-volume inferencing
  • TrustyAI built in — Continuous monitoring for bias, drift, and fairness; LLM evaluation and guardrails for generative AI deployments
  • Enterprise performance — 10.3x more throughput for AI-infused OLTP workloads on IBM z17 RHOAI versus comparable x86

watsonx.ai on IBM Z

IBM watsonx.ai is IBM's AI studio for building, training, fine-tuning, and deploying AI applications — from traditional ML to generative AI. On IBM Z, watsonx.ai acts as the model development and deployment hub, particularly for encoder LLMs and foundation models that are then deployed on-platform via Machine Learning for z/OS or Red Hat OpenShift AI.

What watsonx.ai brings to IBM Z deployment

  • End-to-end AI development — Build and fine-tune models in watsonx.ai, then deploy directly to z/OS or LinuxONE using MLz or RHOAI
  • Foundation model access — Access IBM Granite and partner foundation models; fine-tune them with enterprise data for Z-specific use cases
  • MLOps integration — Integrated with watsonx.governance for model monitoring, factsheets, and lifecycle tracking across hybrid environments
  • LLM portability — Train or fine-tune models in watsonx.ai and import directly into MLz for z/OS deployment
