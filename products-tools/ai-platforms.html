AI on IBM Z

AI Platforms

This page covers the platforms that manage the AI lifecycle on IBM Z, from model training and deployment to governance. Each one covers a different part of that lifecycle, and some overlap. Knowing which platform fits your use case is the first step before looking at frameworks or serving tools.

AI Lifecycle: Build Model · Deploy Models · Serve Model · AI Governance

Machine Learning for IBM z/OS (MLz) is IBM's full-featured AI platform for z/OS — a unified environment for building, deploying, scoring, and governing AI models natively on the mainframe, co-located with the enterprise applications, transaction data, and business logic that already live there. It is designed for data scientists, application developers, and z/OS teams who need to operationalize AI inside CICS transactions, IMS message flows, and Batch jobs — where the data never leaves the platform and response times are measured in milliseconds. The problem MLz solves is fundamental: most AI platforms require data to leave the mainframe for scoring. For banking, insurance, and retail organizations where transactions, customer records, and financial data live on z/OS, that offload introduces latency, increases risk, and complicates governance. MLz brings the model to the data instead.

Key Capabilities

  • End-to-end lifecycle management: train, deploy, version, score, and monitor from one unified UI and REST API
  • Native scoring integration with CICS, IMS, and Batch — in-transaction AI without application re-architecture
  • Direct connections to Db2, IMS, VSAM, and MQ data sources for training and scoring
  • Import models in PMML, ONNX, and Python format; export to standard formats for use elsewhere
  • Telum AI accelerator and zIIP specialty engine exploitation for high-throughput, cost-efficient inference
  • Built-in drift monitoring, bias detection, SHAP explainability, and AI Factsheet integration
  • All model lifecycle events logged and auditable within z/OS security frameworks — data never leaves the boundary

On IBM Z

MLz is the most comprehensive single product for AI on z/OS, spanning Build Model, Deploy Models, Serve Model, and AI Governance. Data scientists can train models in notebooks directly on z/OS via the z/OS Container Extension, using Python, Scikit-learn, XGBoost, or PyTorch against native z/OS data sources. Models trained externally — in watsonx.ai, on a Linux cluster, or in any tool that exports PMML or ONNX — can be imported and deployed on z/OS, making MLz the on-Z deployment and serving target for the broader IBM Z AI ecosystem. Organizations that need real-time AI scoring co-located with their highest-value enterprise data, with full lifecycle governance and without moving data off-platform, should evaluate MLz as their primary AI platform on z/OS.

AI Optimizer for IBM Z and IBM LinuxONE

AI Lifecycle: Deploy Model · Serve Model

AI Optimizer for IBM Z and LinuxONE is an all-in-one inferencing stack designed to simplify the deployment, management, routing, and monitoring of AI workloads on IBM Z. Delivered as a single software appliance, the solution combines the operating system, curated AI models, container runtime, observability tooling, and management interfaces into a single pre-configured environment.

The product serves as the inferencing layer for IBM Spyre Accelerator and helps organizations operationalize enterprise AI workloads closer to mission-critical data and applications. AI Optimizer provides centralized routing, monitoring, model management, and observability capabilities that reduce deployment complexity and accelerate time to value.

AI Optimizer is intended for enterprise architects, AI engineers, application owners, and platform administrators deploying AI at scale on IBM Z. It helps organizations standardize AI inferencing infrastructure while improving visibility, control, and operational efficiency.

Key Capabilities

  • All-in-One Inferencing Stack — Provides a complete, pre-configured AI inferencing environment in a single software appliance.
  • Optimized Inferencing and Intelligent Routing — Routes inference requests across AI model instances using built-in routing strategies and model characteristics. Helps optimize performance and resource utilization.
  • Single Installer Deployment — Simplifies setup through a unified installation and configuration process.
  • Real-Time Monitoring and Observability — Delivers visibility into AI workload performance through built-in monitoring dashboards.
  • Local and External Model Integration — Enables locally deployed and supported remote AI models to be accessed through a common inferencing framework, providing a unified approach to model registration and access.
  • AI Model Deployment and Management — Provides a centralized experience to deploy, configure, update, and manage AI models, including generative AI and encoder-based workloads.
  • IBM Spyre Accelerator Integration — Provides the software layer for running supported AI models with IBM Spyre Accelerator, enabling applications to leverage Spyre acceleration for AI inference workloads.

On IBM Z

AI Optimizer serves as the software layer between AI applications and IBM Spyre Accelerator infrastructure. Applications submit inference requests through AI Optimizer, which manages model selection, routing, monitoring, and observability.

Teams can deploy and manage AI workloads through a pre-configured environment rather than assembling an inferencing stack from individual components. AI Optimizer is particularly valuable when scaling AI workloads into production environments where performance visibility, governance, and operational control are critical.

AI Lifecycle: Build Model · Deploy Models · AI Governance

Red Hat OpenShift AI (RHOAI) is an enterprise AI/ML platform built on Red Hat OpenShift, providing data scientists, ML engineers, and platform teams with everything needed to take a model from experimentation to production — workbench environments, pipeline orchestration, model serving, a model registry, and governance tooling — all packaged as operator-managed services within OpenShift. On IBM Z and LinuxONE, RHOAI runs natively on s390x. It is the right choice for organizations that already standardize on OpenShift on IBM Z and want an AI platform that fits naturally into that infrastructure. The problem it solves is that building production AI systems requires far more than a notebook: you need reproducible pipelines, serving infrastructure, model versioning, governance, and team collaboration tools — and RHOAI provides all of them as a managed, Kubernetes-native service.

Key Capabilities

  • JupyterHub-based data science workbenches on IBM Z hardware with access to Linux on Z data sources
  • Kubeflow Pipelines for building, scheduling, and monitoring reproducible ML workflows
  • Integrated model serving via KServe, IBM Z Accelerated for Triton Inference Server (traditional ML), and Red Hat AI Inference Server (LLMs / foundation models)
  • Centralized model registry for artifact versioning and deployment history
  • TrustyAI for built-in fairness monitoring and drift detection on deployed models
  • Spyre Accelerator support on IBM z17 for LLM inference through the Red Hat AI Inference Server integration
  • Consistent platform across IBM Z on-premises and OpenShift on cloud for hybrid architectures

On IBM Z

RHOAI spans Build Model, Deploy Models, and AI Governance. Data scientists work and train on Z hardware; ML pipelines automate the path from data to deployed model; TrustyAI and watsonx.governance integration enforce governance policies on what's running in production. Organizations that run OpenShift on IBM Z and want a cloud-native, open-standards AI platform — one that runs the same way on Z as it does in the cloud — should evaluate RHOAI as their primary platform. It is the natural home for containerized AI workloads on Linux on IBM Z, particularly generative AI use cases enabled by the IBM Spyre Accelerator on z17.

AI Lifecycle: Prepare Data · Build Model · Deploy Models

IBM watsonx.ai is IBM's enterprise AI studio — a single platform for building, training, fine-tuning, evaluating, and deploying traditional machine learning models and generative AI applications. It is designed for data scientists, ML engineers, and application developers who need a comprehensive environment that handles both the experimentation and production sides of AI development. The problem it solves is fragmentation: building production-quality AI typically requires stitching together too many separate tools — notebooks, training infrastructure, model registries, prompt engineering environments, fine-tuning pipelines, and deployment targets. watsonx.ai consolidates these into one governed, traceable platform, with deployment paths that include IBM Z via Machine Learning for IBM z/OS.

Key Capabilities

  • Curated foundation model library: IBM Granite (text, code, time series), Meta Llama, Mistral, and other open-weight models with enterprise licensing clarity
  • Prompt Lab — interactive prompt engineering, testing, and comparison across supported foundation models
  • Foundation model fine-tuning via LoRA and other parameter-efficient methods on your own labeled data
  • AutoAI — automated model selection, feature engineering, and hyperparameter optimization for traditional ML
  • RAG pipeline construction and tool-calling / agentic workflow development
  • REST API and Python SDK (ibm-watsonx-ai) for programmatic inference, deployment management, and CI/CD integration
  • Built-in connection to watsonx.governance for model lifecycle tracking and compliance metadata

On IBM Z

watsonx.ai covers the Prepare Data, Build Model, and Deploy Models stages. In the IBM Z AI architecture, watsonx.ai is typically where models are built, fine-tuned, and evaluated — and then exported in PMML or ONNX format for deployment on IBM Z via MLz, connecting the cloud AI studio to on-premises Z inference. For developers building generative AI applications that will ultimately score against Z-resident data, the Python SDK (ibm-watsonx-ai) is the primary integration point — supporting ModelInference.generate(), ModelInference.chat(), prompt templates, and agentic tool-calling patterns. Teams evaluating watsonx.ai for IBM Z use cases should start with the tutorials and API reference below.

AI Lifecycle: AI Governance

IBM watsonx.governance is IBM's AI governance platform, built to give organizations visibility, control, and accountability over their AI assets — from traditional ML models to generative AI applications and agentic workflows. It is aimed at AI governance teams, model risk officers, compliance teams, and data scientists who need to ensure their models are fair, explainable, and operating within defined policy boundaries. The core problem it addresses is that as AI expands in the enterprise, deploying a model that works is no longer sufficient — you need to demonstrate that it's working fairly, transparently, and in compliance with internal policy and external regulation. watsonx.governance provides the tooling to do that continuously, not just at deployment time, and extends that discipline to generative AI and agents — not just predictive models.

Key Capabilities

  • AI inventory: centralized register of all use cases, models, prompts, and agents regardless of where they were built or deployed
  • AI Factsheets: automated capture of model metadata, training lineage, evaluation results, deployment history, and operational metrics — assembled into an auditable record throughout the lifecycle
  • Continuous bias and fairness monitoring for production models, with threshold-based alerting
  • SHAP-based model explainability for individual predictions
  • Data drift and performance degradation detection with proactive alerting before model quality degrades
  • GenAI evaluation and guardrails: evaluate prompts and outputs for quality, toxicity, PII, and relevance; gate inputs and outputs in real time
  • Governed agentic catalog for registering, evaluating, and reusing AI agents and tools
  • IBM OpenPages integration for organizations with existing operational risk and compliance workflows

On IBM Z

watsonx.governance is the AI Governance platform for the IBM Z AI stack. Its primary work happens after models are deployed — monitoring, documenting, and enforcing policies on what is running in production. Models deployed on IBM Z via MLz or served through Red Hat OpenShift AI can be registered in watsonx.governance, giving organizations a single pane of glass for AI risk management across on-premises Z and cloud environments. Factsheet capture begins during model development and accumulates through every lifecycle phase, so the governance record is complete before a model reaches production review. Organizations in regulated industries — financial services, healthcare, insurance — where AI governance requirements are rapidly evolving should consider watsonx.governance as the accountability layer across their entire IBM Z AI portfolio.
