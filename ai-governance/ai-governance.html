AI Governance

AI governance is the final — and ongoing — stage of the AI lifecycle. Once a model is in production, you need to monitor it over time, detect when its behavior changes, explain its decisions, and demonstrate compliance with internal policies and external regulations. For IBM Z clients in banking, insurance, healthcare, and the public sector, where regulatory expectations around fairness, explainability, accountability, and auditability are growing, on-platform governance is not optional.

IBM Z's advantage here is the same as every other stage: governance tools run directly on the platform where your data and models live. Data never has to leave IBM Z for monitoring, explainability, or audit — reducing latency, preserving data sovereignty, and keeping everything within the z/OS security boundary.

What happens at this stage?

Monitor model performance

Track model quality and accuracy over time. Detect when a model's predictions begin to diverge from expected behavior — before it affects business outcomes.

Detect drift

Monitor for output drift (predictions changing) and feature drift (input data patterns shifting) — and drill down to identify the source so you can take prompt action.

Explain decisions

Generate explanations for individual model predictions using LIME and SHAP algorithms — supporting auditability, customer transparency, and regulatory requirements.

Ensure fairness and compliance

Identify and monitor for potential bias in model outcomes across demographic or protected groups, and produce documentation for regulatory review and audit.

Who is this stage for?

AI governance is primarily owned by Legal teams and ML Engineers in production, with Data Scientists and Data Engineers as key contributors. The Legal team sets compliance requirements; Data Scientists interpret monitoring results and determine when retraining is needed. On IBM Z, IT Architects are involved in ensuring governance tooling integrates with existing z/OS security and audit infrastructure.

Trustworthy AI in Machine Learning for IBM z/OS

Machine Learning for IBM z/OS includes built-in trustworthy AI capabilities that provide explainability and drift detection for models running natively on z/OS — operating at the individual transaction level without requiring any separate governance platform.

Explainability

MLz provides continuous monitoring of z/OS transactions scored by AI models, and generates explanations for each model prediction at the individual transaction level:

  • Explains why a model made a specific prediction — supporting customer transparency, disputes, and regulatory requirements
  • Supports multiple scoring interfaces: REST, CICS, and WOLA (including IMS transactions and batch jobs)
  • Supports LIME (Local Interpretable Model-agnostic Explanations) and SHAP (SHapley Additive exPlanations) — the most widely used explain algorithms

Drift Detection

MLz monitors both dimensions of model drift in production:

  • Output drift — detects when model predictions start shifting from their baseline distribution, signaling the model may need retraining
  • Feature drift — detects when the input data patterns change, which can cause model behavior to degrade even if the model itself hasn't changed
  • Drill-down capabilities allow data scientists to identify the specific features contributing to drift and take prompt corrective action

These capabilities integrate with watsonx.governance for enterprise-scale governance and compliance workflows.

TrustyAI in Red Hat OpenShift AI

TrustyAI is the model monitoring and governance component built into Red Hat OpenShift AI. It provides continuous monitoring of deployed models — detecting bias, drift, and unreliable predictions — and extends to LLM evaluation and guardrails for generative AI workloads.

Model monitoring capabilities

  • Continuous monitoring — Detects bias, drift, and unreliable predictions across all models deployed in RHOAI
  • Bias and fairness detection — Early detection of bias and drift; ensures transparent, fair, and reliable ML models
  • Explainability insights — Improves AI decision transparency with interpretable outputs
  • Trust and governance — Enhances trustworthiness and accountability in AI systems; supports safer and compliant AI deployments
  • Reduced model risk — Identifies problems in production before they affect business outcomes or compliance standing

LLM evaluation and guardrails

TrustyAI extends governance to generative AI and LLM workloads with two specific assessment services:

LLM Evaluation

Evaluates Large Language Models using multiple quality metrics — ensures output accuracy, consistency, and relevance for production generative AI workloads.

Guardrails

Protects LLM inputs and outputs via a Guardrails Orchestrator that manages flow between user, model, and detector services — flagging sensitive data, harmful content, and toxic outputs before they reach users.
